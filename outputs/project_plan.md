# Old Milton Parkway: transportation capacity and mature-tree preservation

Version 1 | 5 September 2026 | AI-assisted desk study

## Research question
Could a feasible design have met the corridor's transportation needs while preserving more mature trees, and at what incremental cost?

## Completed in this package
- Downloaded the 680-page approved concept-report package and five-page location/design report.
- Examined core narrative, alternatives, cost, geometry, public-involvement, and capacity-summary pages. This is not a visual review of all 680 pages; some attachments are image-only.
- Reconciled the asserted $12 million ROW saving with actual alternative estimates.
- Reproduced historical alternative differences, seven signalized-intersection delay comparisons, and transparent preservation break-even scenarios in executable Python.
- Produced a report and an evidence excerpt packet with original-page mapping.
- Drafted the next records request. No communications have been sent.

## Work plan and completion criteria
### Phase 1: decision audit — completed desk-study version
Question: What was selected, why, and at what estimated cost? Deliverable: report, evidence packet, reproducible model. Completion: every quantitative claim maps to a source page or explicitly labeled assumption. The model is a reanalysis of agency estimates, not an independently calibrated traffic simulation.

### Phase 2: clearing and canopy audit — awaiting final records
Estimated student effort: 8–15 hours after records arrive. Request final clearing/grading/drainage/utility and landscape plans, tree inventories, and the approved environmental document. Obtain dated pre-April-2026 leaf-on imagery with documented resolution and license. Obtain post-clearing imagery with a comparable season; missing imagery is not evidence of zero canopy.

Divide the corridor into the six segments defined by the seven signalized intersections in the report. Add a separate bridge subarea within the first segment without double-counting it. Digitize median canopy and exterior canopy separately in a projected coordinate system with feet/meters, then intersect canopy polygons with actual limits of disturbance. Record imagery date, scale, polygon ID, area, plan sheet and uncertainty. Check a sample twice or with a second reviewer. A canopy polygon is not a tree count.

Classify each tree/cluster: proposed pavement conflict; drainage/utility conflict; grading/root conflict; sight-distance conflict documented by designer; no identified conflict; unknown. Outside the footprint does not automatically mean preservable. Have a qualified arborist or agency reviewer assess likely survival; photograph only from lawful public access away from active works.

Completion: a mapped and auditable estimate of canopy affected, uncertainty, and the reason for removal where documented. Do not promise a count of avoidable removals before this work.

### Phase 3: preservation alternatives — conditional engineering screening
Estimated effort: 8–12 hours plus reviewer availability. Start with one or two candidate clusters, not an invented full redesign. Test localized alignment changes, drainage relocation, and a mixed widening concept against turn-lane storage, sight distance, utilities, grading, bridge geometry, right-of-way, and constructability. A narrower-lane concept requires engineering review; the approved concept already uses 11-foot new lanes. Do not assume all six lanes are 12 feet.

For each candidate record incremental cost (same price year), net additional surviving canopy/trees throughout the footprint, property impacts, and traffic effects. Unknown feasibility remains unknown, not a passing score. Compare only options that meet agreed constraints. Use a Pareto comparison rather than arbitrary weights; a cheaper alternative can still have worse ecological outcomes.

Completion: at least one reviewed candidate or a documented explanation of why evidence is insufficient. An estimate from an engineer is still an estimate, not an approved design.

### Phase 4: validate and communicate
Estimated effort: 3–5 hours. Ask the project team to check disputed facts and reconcile the traffic assumptions and construction-era changes. Incorporate responses with dates. Re-run analysis.py. Write a one-page findings brief and prepare a five-minute explanation of the cost arithmetic and uncertainty. No claim of implementation or reduced tree loss without evidence.

## Decision rule
Tree removal can be necessary for the selected design without being necessary for every feasible design. A project-specific conclusion requires a preservation alternative that satisfies engineering constraints and a net environmental comparison. Until then, the supported finding is that inward widening had a documented economic and construction rationale; universal necessity of clearing is unresolved.
